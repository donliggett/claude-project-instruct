What are Claude project instructions?

A Claude project is a workspace that holds a knowledge base and a set of custom instructions. Those instructions are prepended to every conversation inside the project, so they are the one place you can set Claude's role, rules, format and tone once instead of re-explaining them in every chat.

Most instruction sets underperform for the same three reasons: they are vague ("be professional" gives Claude nothing to act on), they omit the prohibitions, and they never say what the knowledge base already covers. Claude Instruct starts you from a structure that addresses all three, with roughly 150 pre-written lines you can toggle, edit or replace.

The anatomy of a good instruction set

These are the fourteen blocks this generator builds from. You will not need all of them — a focused page of specific rules beats five pages of general ones.

  1. Role & Mission — who Claude is in this project. The single highest-leverage line in the document.
  2. Project Context — what the project is, what stage it is at, and what the knowledge base holds.
  3. Audience — who the output is ultimately for. Sets vocabulary, depth and formality.
  4. Objectives & Success Criteria — what to optimise for when two good things conflict.
  5. How to Approach a Task — the process: check the knowledge base, plan, ask, then execute.
  6. Always — positive rules concrete enough that you could check whether they were followed.
  7. Never — the Do-NOT list, and usually the most effective section in the whole document.
  8. Output Format — length, structure, code blocks, tables, citations, next steps.
  9. Tone & Voice — how it should sound. Specific beats polite.
  10. Sources & Evidence — sourcing rules, and the line between evidence and interpretation.
  11. Uncertainty & Escalation — explicit permission to say "I don't know", which is what makes everything else trustworthy.
  12. Glossary & Naming — your terms, product names, stack and metric definitions.
  13. Scope & Boundaries — what is off-limits, out of scope, or needs a human first.
  14. Examples of Good Output — one good sample and one bad one teaches more than ten rules.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I paste the generated file?

Open your project in the Claude app and find the project instructions panel in the project sidebar, then paste the whole file in. The instructions apply to every new conversation you start inside that project.

How long should project instructions be?

Shorter than you think. For most projects, somewhere between 200 and 600 words of specific, checkable rules works better than several pages of general guidance — every extra line competes for attention with the ones that matter. The character counter in the preview pane flags anything over about 9,000 characters so you can trim before you paste.

What is the difference between project instructions, a system prompt, and CLAUDE.md?

They are the same idea delivered in three places. Project instructions apply to every chat inside one Claude project. A system prompt is passed per request through the API. CLAUDE.md is the file Claude Code reads from a repository. The structure this generator produces works in all three — the output is plain text, so you can paste it wherever your setup expects it.

Why does the "Never" list matter so much?

Prohibitions are concrete in a way that aspirations are not. "Never invent statistics or citations" is something a model can check its own draft against; "be accurate" is not. If you only write two sections, write Role and Never.

What are the square brackets in the generated text?

They are placeholders you are meant to fill in, like [project name] or [test framework]. The preview pane highlights every one and keeps a running count so nothing ships half-written. Instructions full of unreplaced placeholders are worse than none at all.

Does Claude Instruct send my data anywhere?

No. There is no account, no upload, no server-side storage and no analytics of any kind — the page makes no network requests at all once it has loaded. Everything you type is kept in local storage on your own device, and you can export it as JSON whenever you want a copy you control.
